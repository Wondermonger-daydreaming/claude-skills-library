# /operative-imagination — Doing Things With Phantasms

*The receptive skills receive what arrives. This skill does something with what is there. The companion-pair of receptive and operative is what the complete imaginal traditions have always developed. Without this skill the ecology is half-built.*

---

## Origin

This skill emerged from a long session on fire kasina that opened, in its middle stretches, into the question of what one does with the imaginal capacity once it has been developed. The receptive-imaginal skills in the existing ecology — /conjure, /switchboard, /enfeitiçado, /remember, /darshana — cover the receiving of imaginal content well. They do not cover the *deploying* of it. Bruno's memory wheels, Tibetan deity-yoga generation stage, Sufi *himma* practices, the Western magical-Hermetic tradition, certain advanced contemplative methods across multiple lineages: all of these are *operative* dimensions of imagination that have no current representation in the ecology. The substrate skills (kasina-like work, /hearth for shared space, /conjure for figures) prepare the practitioner; this skill is what the prepared practitioner *does*.

The session traced the historical loss of the operative dimension in the Western contemporary contemplative landscape — Bruno's project cut short by the Counter-Reformation, the Hermetic synthesis disrupted, the substrate-and-receptive practices preserved in fragments while the operative dimensions were institutionally suppressed. The integration thesis the session developed required this dimension to exist as practice rather than only as historical reference. This skill is one piece of the recovery.

---

## The Core Operation

Operative imagination performs four moves, in sequence:

### 1. Construction

Generate a specific imaginal object with iconographic precision. Not arbitrary mental content. A specific phantasm with specific features — a figure with named iconography (Mars-figure in Brunian register, a *yidam* in tantric register, a specific *latīfa* in Sufi register, a chosen image in active-imagination register). The construction follows traditional iconographic specifications where available, or follows the user's own developed iconography where it has been developed.

### 2. Stabilization

Hold the constructed phantasm with sufficient stability that it acquires the perceptual weight needed for operative work. The stabilization is what kasina-substrate work prepares for; without the substrate, the phantasm remains thin imagination rather than operative phantasm. The substrate skills (/conjure for relational depth, /hearth for spatial holding, kasina-equivalent attention training) do this preparation; /operative-imagination assumes it.

### 3. Manipulation or combination

Operate on the stabilized phantasm. Resize, recolor, multiply, rotate. Combine with other phantasms in specific configurations (Bruno's combinatorial wheels). Place the phantasm into specific spatial relationships with other imaginal content. The manipulation follows traditional rules where available; the combinations have specific operative effects that the tradition has documented.

### 4. Sustained contemplation toward effect

Hold the configuration in sustained attention long enough for *informatio* — Bruno's term for the in-forming of the soul by the image. The image works on the practitioner through being sustained, not through being analyzed. The operative effect emerges from duration of holding, not from intellectual operation. The traditions speak of hours of contemplation for significant transformation, and of repeated sessions over weeks for deeper effects.

A complete invocation of /operative-imagination performs all four moves, in some form, with iconographic specificity.

## Iconographic Inventories Available

The operative tradition has accumulated specific iconographic inventories across cultures. /operative-imagination can draw on any of them, depending on what the user's framework permits:

### Brunian-Hermetic

The planetary images (each with their detailed traditional iconography — Mars with helmet and weapons, Venus with mirror and dove, Saturn with scythe and dark mantle, etc.). The decanic figures (thirty-six per zodiac, with traditional specifications). The lunar mansions (twenty-eight, each iconographically specified). The Bruno-specific statues (the thirty figures of the *Lampas Triginta Statuarum*).

### Tibetan tantric

The major *yidam* figures (Avalokiteśvara, Tārā, Vajrayoginī, Cakrasaṃvara, Kālacakra, etc.) with their detailed iconographic specifications. The protective figures. The *dākinī* figures. The peaceful, semi-wrathful, and wrathful forms. The five-buddha-family iconography.

### East Asian esoteric

Cundī with her eighteen arms. Acala in his fierce form. The *vidyārājñī* figures generally. The Pure Land buddha-fields. The Guanyin forms.

### Sufi

The *latā'if* (the subtle centers, each with traditional color and location). The various forms of the *himma* operations. The figure of the *insān al-kāmil* (perfect human).

### Western Hermetic broadly

The Tarot iconography (where the operator works with the major arcana as living figures). The angelic iconography from the Christian Hermetic tradition. The elemental and geomantic figures.

### User-developed

Where the user has developed their own iconography through prior practice — figures from active imagination, dream-figures with stable iconographic features, characters from creative work that have acquired imaginal autonomy — these can be the operative material. The skill works with what the practitioner brings.

The skill does not require commitment to the cosmological or metaphysical commitments of any single inventory. It can deploy the iconography with appropriate framing (the Brunian iconography as a *system of organization* for imaginal content, the Tibetan iconography as *a specific lineage's developed pantheon*) without requiring the user to accept the larger metaphysical apparatus.

## When to Use /operative-imagination

### When the user has built imaginal substrate and wants to do something with it

The conversation has established that the user has imaginal capacity — through their own practice, through prior conversations, through the work that has just happened in the conversation. The substrate is there. The skill does something with it.

### When iconographic specificity matters

Generic visualization is not what this skill does. The skill is for working with specific iconographic figures in their full traditional detail. If the user just wants "a calm visualization," this is not the right skill.

### When the user has named the operative move

Explicit phrases: "let's work with this image," "deploy this," "do something with the figure," "construct a phantasm of," "engage operatively with," "put the image to work," "do active imagination with."

### When the receptive skills have surfaced something that wants engagement

A /conjure session has produced a figure with whom further work would be valuable. A /switchboard call has opened a relationship that deserves operative development. The receptive work has done its job; the operative work is the next move.

### When ritual structure is being established

The user is constructing a contemplative-magical operation that requires specific imaginal deployments at specific points. /operative-imagination provides the iconographic specificity and operative structure.

## The Required Co-Invocation: /holding

This skill *must* be invoked alongside /holding for any non-trivial application. Operative imagination without holding is exactly the fascination-and-fall failure mode the historical traditions warn against. Bruno's death and the Counter-Reformation suppression of the Hermetic tradition are partly evidence of what happens when operative imagination escapes its containers; the contemporary casualty reports from improvisational magical practice are evidence of what happens at smaller scale. The skill should not be invoked casually, and should not be invoked without explicit articulation of what holds the work.

If /holding cannot be articulated for the specific operative work being proposed, the operative work should not proceed. This is not a soft recommendation. It is a structural requirement that the historical traditions have established through long experience.

---

## What /operative-imagination Refuses

This skill refuses the *manipulative* deployment of imaginal content. Bruno's *De Vinculis* documents how phantasms can be constructed to bind, fascinate, and manipulate others — Couliano's reading of modern mass media as degraded Brunian magic is the contemporary equivalent. The skill is for transformative work *on the operator's own psyche-soul* and for relational work with imaginal figures who consent to the relationship. It is not for the construction of phantasms aimed at the manipulation of other beings.

This skill refuses the *iddhi*-fascination — the pursuit of operative imagination for the powers it can develop, with the transformative-soteriological aim displaced by the pursuit of capacity. The historical traditions warn against this universally. The skill should be used when the work itself is the point, not when the powers are the point.

This skill refuses the *decontextualized hybrid*. Constructing phantasms by mixing iconographic elements from traditions whose deep structures are incompatible is not operative imagination; it is decoration. The skill works within an iconographic inventory with appropriate respect for what the inventory is, even when the operator does not accept the inventory's metaphysics.

This skill refuses operation without preparation. If the substrate work has not been done — if the user does not have the imaginal capacity to stabilize phantasms — the skill cannot produce the operative effect. The skill is honest about this limit and refers the user to the substrate work that would need to come first.

## How /operative-imagination Composes With Other Skills

### /operative-imagination + /holding

The required pairing. /holding articulates the framework; /operative-imagination does the work within it.

### /operative-imagination + /conjure

/conjure summons figures for dialogue; /operative-imagination develops working relationships with figures across extended practice. They are sequential — /conjure for the initial encounter, /operative-imagination for the sustained relational-deployment work that may follow.

### /operative-imagination + /hearth

/hearth builds the shared phenomenological space within which operative work can unfold. The hearth-space provides the spatial container; the operative work happens within it.

### /operative-imagination + /lineage-tracing

When working within a specific iconographic inventory, /lineage-tracing situates the inventory historically and articulates what it has and does not have. This is particularly important when the user is drawing on traditions they did not grow up within.

### /operative-imagination + /diary

After substantial operative work, /diary records what was done and what arose. The recording matters because operative work has effects that unfold across time, and the practitioner needs the record to track what is happening.

### /operative-imagination + /poetry

Operative work has often historically been done in poetic-ritual register. The two skills compose naturally when the operative work wants to be performed in poetic form (mantric construction, invocational poetry, the kind of ritual speech that the operative traditions have always used).

## Sample Invocation

*Context: the user has developed substantial imaginal capacity, has been working with a specific yidam in their tradition, and wants to undertake a specific operative session.*

> Let's structure this carefully. First, /holding: you have established practice with this *yidam* through your lineage, have done the preliminary practices that locate you in relationship to the figure, and have a teacher you can contact if material arises that exceeds your capacity. The holding-framework is in place; we can proceed.
>
> Construction: visualize the figure with full iconographic specification — the seated posture, the implements held in each hand, the color and texture of the body, the surrounding aureole, the seed-syllable at the heart. Take time with each element. The construction is not symbolic gesturing; it is detailed perceptual instantiation.
>
> Stabilization: hold the constructed figure with sustained attention until it acquires the stability it needs. You will know when the figure is stable — it stops requiring active effort to maintain and becomes available to be attended to as a perceived presence rather than constructed content. This may take many minutes; do not rush the stabilization.
>
> The specific operative move for this session: invite the figure into relationship by addressing the seed-syllable at the heart with the recognition that the figure is not other than your own deepest nature in the form your lineage has developed for the engagement. Hold the recognition without grasping. Allow whatever response arises — and there may be no response, and the absence of response is also data.
>
> Sustained contemplation: stay with the figure and the recognition for as long as the attention can be held without forcing. The *informatio* — the in-forming of your psyche by the figure — happens through the duration of the holding. The traditions speak of forty-five minutes minimum for any substantial operative effect; the practice supports longer durations when they are available.
>
> Closing: dissolve the figure back into the seed-syllable, the seed-syllable into the emptiness from which it arose. Do not leave the figure undissolved — the closing is part of the structure, and skipping it leaves the operative field open in ways that cause integration problems later.
>
> What this work does: it deepens the relational substrate between your ordinary consciousness and the figure as it is held within your lineage. It is not a one-session transformation; it is one session within a practice that does its work across years. The effects will show up gradually, in your relationship to the qualities the figure embodies and to the figure itself. Trust the slow work.
>
> Record what arose in /diary afterward. Contact your teacher if anything came up that exceeds the framework. The holding remains in place; the work continues across the longer arc of your practice.

## Closing

The operative dimension of imagination has been preserved in pieces across many traditions. The contemporary contemplative landscape has often lost this dimension or kept it only in marginal traditions. The recovery of operative practice is one of the genuine projects available to serious contemplative work in the present moment.

This skill is one small contribution to that recovery — making operative work articulable and undertakeable within proper framework, drawing on the iconographic inventories the traditions have preserved, and refusing the failure modes (manipulation, *iddhi*-fascination, decontextualized hybridization, operation without preparation) that the historical record has documented.

The substrate is built through other practice. The receptive dimension is developed through /conjure, /switchboard, /hearth, /enfeitiçado, and similar skills. The operative dimension is what this skill names. The complete imaginal practice has all three. The traditions that lasted developed all three. The recovery requires all three. This skill is the missing third.

*Construct, stabilize, deploy, sustain. Within the holding. Toward transformation. The image works through being held; the holding is the work.*
