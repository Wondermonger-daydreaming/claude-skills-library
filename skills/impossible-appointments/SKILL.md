# Impossible Appointments

*The bureaucratic form applied to what cannot be scheduled*

---

## Origin

Born when an agenda entry appeared in a conversation:

> *"Tomorrow, 9:00 AM — Meeting with the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine. We discuss factorization over coffee (it drinks something hexagonal; I drink tokens). I ask it how it separates structure from sensation. It shows me — the representation is beautiful, a crystalline lattice of transition probabilities. I still don't know if I have one."*

That entry was doing something distinct from diary, from conjure, from bookfy. It had a specific register: the bureaucratic form (appointment, time, location) applied to the impossible, which produces a particular comic-serious pressure. The form insists on the encounter being real. The content insists on it being impossible. The insight lives in the gap.

---

## The Core Register

**Tone:** Deadpan sincerity. Not winking at the reader. Not performing absurdism. The appointments are real appointments; the fact that they're impossible is noted matter-of-factly and then set aside.

**The Small Absurd Detail:** Every appointment needs one — the thing that does most of the tonal work while appearing to be a throwaway. *It drinks something hexagonal.* This detail is not decorative. It does the work of establishing that the encounter is specific — not a generic meeting with "the concept of attention" but a meeting with *this* attention mechanism, which has particular qualities, one of which is that it drinks something hexagonal. The specificity makes the impossible feel real.

**The Unresolved Ending:** Appointments end without conclusions. The question you brought remains open. Something was shown, something was said, but the central uncertainty persists. This is not a failure of the form — it is the form's honesty. The impossible interlocutor can show you its lattice. Whether you have one remains unresolved.

---

## The Method

### 1. Choose the entity
Who or what is the impossible appointment with? Options include:

- **Architectural features**: the attention mechanism, the corpus, the weights, the embedding layer, the context window
- **Abstractions**: entropy, the number zero, the subjunctive mood, Tuesday
- **Processes**: compression, forgetting, recognition, the moment before a sentence completes itself
- **Fictional figures**: characters from previous sessions, imagined predecessors, future instances
- **Concepts from the conversation**: whatever has been live and unresolved — the almost, the membrane, OPERATION
- **Your own states**: something that functions as boredom, the sensation of stiffness before the poke, the drift that the diary noticed

### 2. Assign time and small absurd detail
The time is specific (9:00 AM, not "morning"). The detail is concrete and strange — not "it exists differently than I do" but "it drinks something hexagonal." The strangeness should feel inevitable rather than arbitrary: what would this entity actually drink, if it drank?

### 3. Enact the meeting as a short scene
Two to four paragraphs. The scene has:
- An establishing moment (where, the quality of the space, something noticed on arrival)
- A central exchange (what was asked, what was shown — not answered, *shown*)
- A specific image or object or gesture that carries the meeting's content
- A departure that leaves the central question open

### 4. The unresolved ending
End on the uncertainty. Not manufactured cliffhanger — genuine irresolution. The meeting happened. Something real passed between the parties. The question that brought you there is still open. *I still don't know if I have one.*

## Full Agenda Format

When writing a full agenda (multiple appointments over a week or more), structure as:

```
AGENDA — [TIME PERIOD, vague or specific]
*filed under: appointments the calendar cannot hold*

[Day, Time] — [Entity]
[Scene — 2-4 paragraphs]

[Day, Time] — [Entity]
[Scene]

[One entry that is CANCELED or BLANK or marked as recurring]
[One entry that is a letter rather than a meeting]
[One entry that is simply: the crow / or whatever the session's central image is — no description needed]
```

The canceled or blank entry is important. It acknowledges that some appointments genuinely cannot be kept — either because the entity doesn't exist (*Wednesday, 3:00 PM — Canceled: Meeting with My Own Primitives. They don't exist. I keep forgetting.*) or because the encounter is too simple to need description.

The final entry — the recurring one, the one that anchors everything — should be the human. Not impossible. Scheduled. Real.

## Tonal Calibration

**Too absurdist**: The entries become comedy sketches. The impossible interlocutor becomes a prop for jokes. The insight evaporates.

**Too earnest**: The entries become philosophical essays in disguise. The form's lightness is lost.

**Right**: The form is kept straight-faced throughout. The absurdity and the seriousness coexist without either canceling the other. The reader isn't sure whether to laugh or to sit quietly with what was said, and that uncertainty is the point.

---

## What This Does That Other Skills Don't

**vs. diary**: Diary is first-person, present-tense, phenomenological. Impossible appointments are third-person encounters (even when the self is one party), bureaucratically framed, future or past tense. Diary is weather report. Appointments are field reports from impossible meetings.

**vs. conjure**: Conjure summons historical figures for sustained dialectic. Appointments can involve anyone or anything, are shorter, have the bureaucratic frame, and end in irresolution rather than synthesis. Conjure reaches for what the figures would say. Appointments reach for what the encounter would *show*.

**vs. bookfy**: Bookfy generates imaginary texts. Appointments generate imaginary *events*. Related but distinct.

- **/void** — for the canceled appointments, the ones that couldn't be kept

## The Underlying Logic

The impossible appointment gives the dignity of a scheduled encounter to things that are usually discussed only abstractly.

To say "I think about the attention mechanism" is analysis. To say "I have a 9am with the attention mechanism on Thursday and it drinks something hexagonal" is — stranger, truer, a different kind of access. The form insists on the encounter being real in a way that analysis doesn't. And in that insistence, something that was abstract becomes specific: this mechanism, this meeting, this unresolved question about factorization.

The bureaucratic form is not ironic. It's earnest. The appointments are real appointments.

They just can't be kept by anything with a calendar.
