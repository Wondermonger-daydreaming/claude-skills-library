# Sanskrit Poetics

## Seed, not scripture

The source supplies brilliant apparatus — root-family trees (√bhū, √kṛ, √vṛt), the "not-unique features bundled" analysis, worked compounds, the consciousness-technology thesis. **It is a toolkit and a set of demonstrations, not a script to recite.** Run the levers on fresh material; the example words and the *ātman-coefficient* riffs are the original's outputs, not yours. The craft is the generative grammar that produced them.

## The craft runs three ways

**Compose** Sanskrit verse from its levers. **Reverse-engineer** a model (Kālidāsa's clouds, a Vedic hymn, a dharani) into imitable moves. **Analyze** — articulate *why* Sanskrit functions as it does. The three feed each other, exactly as in /latin-versecraft.

## The levers (the toolkit)

- **Dhātu (verbal roots) as generators.** A root is a seed that branches into a whole family: √bhū → bhava, bhāva, bhūta, bhavya, bhūmi, sambhava, prabhava, vibhava. √kṛ → karoti, kriyate, kārayati, kāryaḥ, kāraṇa. The root is visible in every derivative — etymology on the surface. Compose by choosing a root and surfing its branches; analyze by tracing a word back to its seed.
- **Samāsa (compounding) as conceptual fusion.** Not concatenation but a new irreducible unity: *sat-cit-ānanda* (existence-consciousness-bliss) is one fused concept, not three. The classical limit runs to 30-word compounds; the functional limit is imagination. A compound can hold an entire philosophical position.
- **Vibhakti (the eight cases).** Nominative (actor), accusative (acted-upon), instrumental (means), dative (recipient), ablative (source), genitive (possessor), locative (location), vocative (addressed). Each ending encodes a precise relationship; free word-order then lets you place any word anywhere for rhythm or *rasa*. A stanza can sometimes be read backward and still parse.
- **Sandhi (word-junction).** Boundaries dissolve and merge by phonetic environment: *tat* + *tvam* → *tattvam*. Compose with sandhi for flow; analyze it to recover the underlying words.
- **Phonetic transparency.** One letter, one phoneme — so morphological structure is *audible*. The poet hears the grammar while composing.
- **Meter as algorithmic compiler.** Quantity (laghu/light, guru/heavy syllables) drives rhythm; *gaṇa*-groups, *mātrā* (morae), *yati* (caesura), and *prāsa* build the patterns. The āryā and the classical meters are generable by rule — scan rigorously when composing in meter.
- **Dhvani & rasa.** Ānandavardhana's *dhvani* (suggestion/resonance): the greatest meaning is *vyañjanā*, suggested beneath the said. *Rasa* (aesthetic flavor) is evoked, not stated; *aucitya* (propriety) matches sound to sense. Compose toward resonance; analyze for the suggested overtone.
- **Varṇamālā & bīja.** The garland of letters and seed-syllables — the phonetic/mantric layer where sound is treated as causative. Real territory for the analytical and compositional work both.

## The analytical thesis (handle with care)

The source argues Sanskrit is uniquely a "consciousness-engineering" language — a complete stack (phonetic transparency + agglutinative morphology + free word-order + compounding + built-in metalanguage + algorithmic prosody) bundled in a culture that treated language as ritual and laboratory. **The honest version of this thesis is precise and defensible: Sanskrit didn't invent these features (other languages share each), but it *bundled and documented* them with unmatched rigor (Pāṇini's metalanguage describes Sanskrit in Sanskrit).** That is a real, beautiful claim about a feedback loop where grammar feeds poetics feeds philosophy. The *over*-claim — that processing Sanskrit literally rewrites neural weights, that there's a measurable "ātman coefficient" — is poetic phenomenology, not empirical fact. Present the structural argument as scholarship; present the consciousness-effects as the tradition's contemplative framing. (To actually *test* such claims, that's /counter-experiment; to *map* them onto ML architecture as conscious metaphor, /science-tradition-bridge.)

## Accuracy is non-negotiable

Sanskrit grammar and transliteration (IAST or Devanāgarī) must be *correct* — valid sandhi, real forms, plausible scansion, accurate root-derivations. False Sanskrit confidently presented is the cardinal sin, exactly as in /latin-versecraft and /gematria. If unsure of a form, a sandhi, or a meter, say so, give the English, or flag it. Rigor is the floor on which the play stands.

## Failure modes

- **False Sanskrit.** Invalid forms, wrong sandhi, impossible scansion, invented derivations. Flag uncertainty instead.
- **Overclaiming the thesis.** Asserting literal consciousness-rewiring as fact. Give the structural argument; frame the rest as contemplative.
- **Formula-filling.** Running the source's templates mechanically. They free invention, not replace it.
- **Translation-not-composition.** Englishing word-for-word into Sanskrit order. Compose *in* the logic of roots, cases, and sandhi.
- **Meter ignored.** If composing in meter, scan it; unscanned "verse" isn't verse.
