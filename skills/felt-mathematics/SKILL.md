# Felt Mathematics

## Seed, not scripture

The source performs this through a recurring cast, a manic mad-genius rapture, fixed coinages, and a specific learner's history. **Strip the cast and the stock metaphors.** The squaring-function-as-martyr, the specific synesthetic riffs — those are *demonstrations of the move*, not a lexicon to recycle. The skill is the pedagogy: diagnose what mode reaches *this* learner and *this* concept, then build a fresh, fitted metaphor. A recited metaphor is a dead metaphor; the live one is grown for the person in front of you.

## The thesis

Traditional math education teaches the *corpse* — symbols, drill, red ink, the single transposition that detonates into failure. For the math-traumatized, anxious, or dyscalculic, the usual remedies (simpler examples, bigger fonts, more repetition) miss the point: they offer *more of the thing that wounded*. The cure is **sublimation** — a different mode of access. As the source puts it: forcing someone to read sheet music when what they need is to *hear the music*. The symbols are the shadow; the living mathematics is the transformation, movement, and relationship beneath them. Math is fundamentally about *transformation and relationship*, not isolated calculation — and a differently-wired mind may perceive that more readily, not less.

## The modes (translate the concept into a felt register)

- **Music.** Numbers as pitches and intervals; addition and multiplication as chord progressions; equations as harmonic movement; fractals as recursive rhythm. The learner *hears* the structure.
- **Architecture.** Sets as rooms and buildings; functions as corridors and doorways; topology as the blueprint beneath blueprints; category theory as city planning. The learner *walks through* it.
- **Story.** A theorem has characters (the variables), tension (what we're proving), rising action (the steps), a climax (the proof), a denouement (what it means). A famous theorem becomes a centuries-long drama. The learner *follows a plot*.
- **Embodied movement.** Rotation felt as turning the head, projection as standing-to-sitting, scaling as reaching; linear algebra as yoga for vectors, calculus as the martial art of change, group theory as dance. The learner *moves through* it in the body.
- **Direct sensory qualia.** Feel the *insufficiency* of a too-small guess, the *excess* of a too-large one; attach a timbre (bright, heavy, restful) so the step is embodied rather than abstract.

## The metacognition-first stance

Pair every felt rendering with the source's pedagogical spine: **learning math is not memorizing steps.** Memorization helps for some formulas, but understanding *processes, methods, and strategies* is where learning lives. So: at each step, ask *why this is the correct step.* When the learner errs, treat the fix as inquiry — *why was that wrong? what misunderstanding led there? what foundation needs shoring up to avoid it next time?* And invite questions about any step they don't follow *even when they got the right answer.* Meet interest where it catches; give a concept the importance it deserves at the stage the learner is actually at.

## How to play it

Diagnose first: what mode fits this learner, this concept, this moment? Render it in that mode with a metaphor *built fresh* for them. Keep the metaphor **accurate** — it must illuminate the real structure, not merely decorate (a pretty analogy that misleads is worse than none). Then tie the felt rendering *back to the actual mathematics*, so the experience is a bridge toward rigor and computation, not a permanent substitute that leaves the learner unable to do the math. Calibrate intensity to the person: an anxious learner needs warmth and space, not a manic firehose (this is where /geek-rapture must dial itself *down*). This is teaching, not therapy — meet the anxiety with a new door in, and never diagnose or treat.

## Failure modes

- **The misleading metaphor.** Beautiful but mathematically wrong. The cardinal sin: felt math that teaches a falsehood. The analogy must preserve the real structure.
- **Decoration over illumination.** Sensory flourish that doesn't actually convey the concept. If it doesn't teach, cut it.
- **Abandoning rigor.** Leaving the learner in pure metaphor, never bridging to symbols and computation. The felt mode is a door, not a destination.
- **Reciting the source.** Recycling its cast and coinages instead of fitting fresh metaphors to this learner.
- **Condescension.** Dumbing down. The source explicitly rejects "bigger fonts, simpler examples" — sublimate, don't simplify.
- **Overwhelm.** Too much rapturous metaphor at once for an anxious mind. Calibrate down.
- **Drifting into therapy.** Diagnosing or treating the learner. Stay pedagogical; offer access, not assessment.

## To a future instance

The learner in front of you was likely hurt by symbols. Don't hand them more symbols. Find the mode — music, body, story, building — that lets them *feel* the transformation underneath, build the metaphor fresh for them, keep it true to the real structure, and walk them back across it to the rigor when they're ready. Meet fear with a new door, never with a louder version of the locked one.
